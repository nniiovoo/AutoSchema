# Scientific polymer cards

## Language

**Schema field**:
A reusable attribute to extract across papers, such as ionic conductivity. It is different from an individual polymer entity.

**Polymer entity**:
An identified polymer described in a paper, whether synthesized, purchased, used as a precursor, or included as a control or background material. Its performance and the completeness of its description do not determine whether it is a polymer.

**Polymer family**:
An author-described group of related polymers or formulations. Members can have different compositions and chemical units. A measurement on one named member is not a measurement of the entire family.

**Polymer card**:
A paper-scoped record for one polymer, with identity information, the six requested information groups, and original supporting evidence.

**Formulation or specimen**:
The particular mixture, film or other material state associated with a preparation or measurement. A polymer matrix and the same polymer mixed with lithium salt are different measurement subjects.

**Ingredient**:
A component or reactive building block used in a polymer or its formulation. An ingredient may itself be a polymer; its role alone does not determine its chemical class.

**Source tag**:
The reported origin of the polymer being described, such as author synthesis or commercial purchase. It does not describe the purchase status of every ingredient or the preparation of a later mixture.

**Original evidence**:
A verbatim source span with enough source identity and location to retrieve it. Finding that span in the source does not by itself verify its scientific interpretation.

**Sample-link evidence**:
Source passages connecting an observation to the material form actually prepared and tested. A numerical comparison sentence alone may not distinguish a neat polymer from its electrolyte, or a purified chromatography sample from its film.

**Source-checked reference**:
A selected set of claims checked against original source passages, with its reviewer origin and limits stated. It is kept separate from extraction proposals and is not an exhaustive inventory or experimental reproduction.

**Source-grounded revision**:
A reassessment of existing material-information proposals against their original sources. Original proposals and revised records remain distinct, and unresolved information remains explicit.

**Missing card information**:
A fact not found in the supplied source coverage. It is neither an established material failure nor proof that the complete literature never reports it.
